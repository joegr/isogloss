An accent is fourteen numbers. Move one and listen to what changes.

Press Speak, or hit space.

What you are hearing. A Klatt-style formant synthesiser, running entirely in this tab. It sounds like 1980s speech synthesis because that is what it is — the point is not realism but control: every parameter here is a quantity the analyser measures, so the same fourteen numbers describe an accent and generate one.

What you are seeing. The circle is driven by the synthesiser's own parameter track. Hue is the second formant, so the colour is the front/back axis of the vowel space: front vowels burn amber, back vowels go cold blue. Lobes are formants, spin is pitch, the barb on one side is r-colouring, the pixel storm is frication, and each ghost ring is one glottal pulse.

Caveat. The preset varieties are expert approximations synthesised from the descriptive literature — Wells' lexical sets, the Atlas of North American English, the Survey of English Dialects — not measurements from a corpus. They reproduce the well-known isoglosses; they are not evidence.
